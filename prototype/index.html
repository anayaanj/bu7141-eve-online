02 · Newcomers arrive alone, and leave

Of 100 newcomers, 99 are gone within a year. Most leave after one day, alone.

100 sign up · of 100 who sign up

New characters, Jan 2024 – Aug 2026: 13 in 100 are ever seen fighting or trading with another player; 8.2% of those are still playing a year later. CCP's own figure (2019): 9 in 10 new players quit in their first week.

Their first month · of 100 we can see

The 13 we can see, scaled to 100. Most appear on a single day, and for most of them the only trace is being killed by another player.

Seen on more days, seen again later · of 100 we can see

62% are seen on a single day; 9.5% of them are seen again 3 months later, against 45% of those seen on 8+ days.

Where the one-day newcomers die · killed by another player

Safe at home: 0.15% die in a starter system (white rings). They die after heading out alone; about 1 in 6 in just five systems. The official tutorial and career missions are solo, against the computer.

05 · Recommendation

Put newcomers in a group in week one. Then test it.

What if more newcomers found a group?

What to do

  • End the tutorial in the corporation finder, not in solo missions: route newcomers from the starter corporation into a mid-sized player corporation.
  • Give them a first fight they can win, with a group.
  • Spend on keeping as well as buying: a newcomer in a group pays back their cost in year one; one alone covers about 60%.
  • A/B test: half of new players end the tutorial in the corporation finder, half in today's solo missions; measure month-3 return and first-year revenue.

Caveats

  • We see 13% of new characters in detail: those who fight or trade. Signups, players online and revenue cover everyone.
  • "One-day" means seen on one day, not played on one day. Some play quietly and only appear when killed. Activity is killmails and contracts, not logins.
  • A link, not proof. Keen players may join groups more often; the equal-activity check and the Horde case reduce but don't remove this.
  • Money figures are estimates and upper bounds: all revenue is put on visible players, and marketing covers all of CCP. The comparison between groups is the point.
  • Characters, not people. One account can have up to three characters, and a player can have several accounts.
